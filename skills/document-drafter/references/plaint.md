# Plaint — Template

## Output Category

**Category B — Court Document.** Begin output with:
`[COURT DOCUMENT — No letterhead. File at registry. Check case number before filing.]`

---

## When to Use

A Plaint is the originating document for civil suits commenced in the High Court or Magistrate's Court under the Civil Procedure Act and Civil Procedure Rules, 2010. It is the correct vehicle for:
- Debt recovery and breach of contract claims
- Tort claims (negligence, conversion, defamation, trespass)
- Land and property disputes (if in the Magistrate's Court — disputes over land go to the ELC at the High Court level)
- General money claims
- Probate and succession suits (commenced by Originating Summons or Petition — not a Plaint)

**Do not use a Plaint for:**
- Constitutional petitions — use a Petition
- Judicial review — use Misc. Application under Order 53
- Appeals — use a Memorandum of Appeal
- Employment disputes — commence in the ELRC under its own rules

A Plaint must comply with **Order 4** of the Civil Procedure Rules, 2010.

## Template Addition — Verification

Under Order 4 Rule 1(1) CPR 2010, the Plaint must be **verified by the Plaintiff** (or, for a company, by a duly authorised officer). Append this below the Plaint:

```
VERIFICATION

I, [PLAINTIFF FULL NAME / AUTHORISED OFFICER NAME AND DESIGNATION], do hereby verify that the contents of this Plaint are true and correct to the best of my knowledge, information, and belief.

_______________________
[PLAINTIFF / AUTHORISED OFFICER]
DATED this [DAY] day of [MONTH], [YEAR]
```

## Key Drafting Rules (Order 4, CPR 2010)

- **Order 4 Rule 1:** The Plaint must contain: the cause of action, the names/addresses of the parties, the facts constituting the cause of action and when it arose, the relief sought, and the value of the subject matter (for jurisdictional purposes).
- **Jurisdiction:** State the value or subject matter to establish that the correct court is seized. High Court — unlimited civil jurisdiction. Chief Magistrate — up to Kshs. 20 million. Senior Resident Magistrate — up to Kshs. 10 million (check current limits under the Magistrates' Courts Act, 2015).
- **One fact per paragraph.** Short, numbered paragraphs — never mix facts.
- **Annexures:** Label as "P.1", "P.2" etc. (Plaintiff's exhibits). List each annexure in the paragraph where it is first mentioned.
- **Particulars:** Where claiming damages, always include a Particulars section — courts will strike out unparticularised damage claims.
- **Verification:** Non-negotiable. An unverified Plaint is defective and may be struck out on application.
- **Limitation periods:** Before drafting, always check the Limitation of Actions Act (Cap. 22). General contract/tort — 6 years. Land — 12 years. Personal injury — 3 years. Government — file within the applicable period after the 30-day demand notice.
